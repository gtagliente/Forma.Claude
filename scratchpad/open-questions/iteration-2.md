# Open Questions — Iteration 2

Surfaced by Challenger review of ADR-006/`integration-patterns.md`/the Iteration-2 Analyst cross-context-reference-integrity section (see `../challenger-review-iteration-2.md` for full reasoning). Unresolved as of this pass — for the next Analyst/Architect iteration or human product-owner input.

## High priority (affects whether ADR-006 should be accepted as-is)

1. **Should fail-closed on Exercise-delete's reference check be scoped to shared Exercises only, rather than applied uniformly to private ones too?** For a private Exercise, the deleting user is by construction the only person who can ever be affected by its references (Analyst's own text) — blocking their delete during a `training-planning-service` outage protects nobody but themselves. A shared-only fail-closed policy (private Exercises fail-open-with-warning, or a softer confirm-and-proceed flow) would cut the availability blast radius substantially while preserving the strict guarantee exactly where the Analyst's flagged shared/private asymmetry says it matters. Not addressed in ADR-006's "Alternatives considered." See challenger review §1.
2. **No observability requirement exists for either check's inconclusive/fail-open/fail-closed outcomes.** Fail-open is silent by design; without a logged/metriced rate of inconclusive results, a persistently broken check (not a transient outage) is indistinguishable from normal operation and could quietly undermine rule 1's "catch the common case early" goal indefinitely, or (in dev/CI, where `exercise-service` is often simply not running) make the check appear to work in every environment where it's actually never exercised. Needs a decision before either check ships, not after. See challenger review §2.

## Medium priority (shapes how cleanly this scales to the next edge)

3. **No concrete trip-wire for revisiting the orchestrator rejection.** "Revisit if the graph densifies" has no owner or threshold. Recommend defining one explicitly (e.g. "revisit when a third service needs an existence/reference check against either `exercise-service` or `training-planning-service`") so the deferral doesn't become permanent by inertia. See challenger review §3.
4. **`integration-patterns.md`'s per-edge contract (mechanism + failure mode) is recorded as prose tied to these two specific drivers, not as a maintained, reusable registry.** Recommend a living table (edge / caller→callee / business rule / mechanism / failure-mode-on-inconclusive) that every future cross-service check gets a row in, so the next edge (User/`OwnerId`, or whatever comes first) doesn't require re-deriving the pattern from scratch. See challenger review §3.
5. **How is each check mocked/stubbed for local dev and CI when the other service isn't running?** Neither `integration-patterns.md` nor either service's `open-questions.md` states this. For rule 2 in particular (fail-closed), this affects whether `exercise-service`'s delete feature can be meaningfully developed/tested at all without standing up `training-planning-service` alongside it. See challenger review §2.
6. **The "User/`OwnerId` fan-out" framing likely bundles two structurally different problems.** Per-create "does this User exist" is arguably redundant with authentication (the caller is already proven to be that User) and may not need a live existence check at all; account-deletion reference-checking is a real, but low-frequency, cascading concern closer to a one-time fan-out job than a per-request check. Recommend splitting this into two explicit questions before `identity-service`'s own central-loop pass starts, rather than carrying it forward as one bundled "near-certain next occurrence." See challenger review §5.

## Lower priority (worth bundling into an existing deferred item, not new work)

7. **Shared-library governance (Users, item 3) and the "shared Exercise may become undeletable" tension (requirements-and-open-items.md open question 3) are tightly coupled but currently tracked as separate deferred items.** Recommend whichever iteration picks up either one explicitly picks up both together — deciding who can delete/curate a shared Exercise and what happens when the delete is blocked are not independently decidable. See challenger review §6.

## Addendum — Client-generation tooling (surfaced 2026-07-12, see challenger review's "Addendum review" section)

### High priority (affects whether the addendum should be accepted as-is)

8. **Refit + Refitter's manual regeneration has no drift-detection mechanism** (no lock file, no committed spec-hash, no diff check), unlike Kiota's `kiota-lock.json`. Combined with the document's own mitigation for Refitter's smaller/newer maintenance trajectory ("output is plain, hand-maintainable C# if the generator stalls"), an accidental stale-regen and a deliberate hand-maintenance divergence are indistinguishable by construction — this is a sharper, more concrete version of the human owner's question 2 (CI-gated regeneration) than the document currently treats it as. Recommend either a lightweight non-CI diff-check script before it ships, or explicitly naming this as a known, accepted gap the way the security note does for auth. See challenger review "Addendum review" §1.
9. **The security note's framing scopes the auth gap to "the two ADR-006 endpoints" when it is actually a system-wide, every-endpoint-in-every-service gap** — confirmed by repo-wide search, this addendum is the first place in the whole documentation set that states the system has no authentication anywhere, but its wording undersells that scope in a way that could lead a future reader (e.g. whoever runs `identity-service`'s first central-loop pass) to under-scope the remediation. Recommend restating the system-wide fact first, then narrowing to these two endpoints as one instance of it; also recommend a pointer to this fact be placed somewhere central (e.g. `identity-service/open-questions.md`) rather than left discoverable only inside this specific addendum subsection. See challenger review "Addendum review" §5.

### Medium priority

10. **Network topology (public ingress vs. private/internal-only) for the two ADR-006 endpoints is not stated**, and materially changes the real severity of the unauthenticated-calls gap — "anyone who can reach the network path" means very different things depending on which applies. Should be stated, or explicitly flagged as unknown rather than left implicit. See challenger review "Addendum review" §5.
11. **The ADR README's own written inclusion criteria ("affects more than one service/feature" is sufficient on its own, per the "or") arguably already qualify this decision for `adr/`**, even though it's a non-binding recommendation — the addendum's justification for staying out of `adr/` is a new, unwritten exception not currently sanctioned by `adr/README.md`'s text. Recommend either adding an explicit "non-binding shared recommendation" carve-out to `adr/README.md`, or recording this as ADR-007 with "Status: Accepted (non-binding default)" — either closes the gap; leaving it as an undocumented self-exemption risks becoming ungoverned precedent for the next gray-area case. See challenger review "Addendum review" §3.
12. **The "not centrally mandated" reasoning leans on a precedent (intra-service provisional defaults like `DayOfWeek?`) that isn't a clean match for this case** — those are single-service, single-author defaults about domain behavior; this is a two-service, same-team, bidirectionally-coupled tooling choice. The document's stronger argument (invisible to the callee's build, no shared artifact) still holds and isn't undermined by this — but the precedent-matching paragraph shouldn't be relied on the next time a genuinely two-party tooling question comes up (e.g. shared contract testing, correlation-ID conventions) where consistency plausibly matters more. See challenger review "Addendum review" §2.
13. **Kiota's forward-fit for Identity/`OwnerId` fan-out is being weighed against a premise (the fan-out framing) this same review cycle already disputes** (see item 6 above / challenger review §5) — the client-generation addendum doesn't cross-reference that dispute, so a reader of just that section would take "near-certain next occurrence" at face value. Recommend a one-line cross-reference once the fan-out split (item 6) is resolved. Net effect strengthens, not weakens, the Refit pick — just currently unstated. See challenger review "Addendum review" §4.

### Not re-raised (checked, holds up)

- .NET version divergence between the two services as a risk to client-generation tooling choice — checked; generation is per-project against that project's own TFM, no shared artifact for a version mismatch to break. Not a real risk.
- Refit vs. Kiota as the primary axis of concern — the ports-and-adapters/output-shape reasoning for picking Refit today is sound; the sharper risk is regen-drift tooling (item 8), not generator identity.

## Not re-raised (checked, holds up)

- Orchestrator vs. mesh for the current graph shape — Architect's analysis is accurate; no orchestrator needed today.
- Async events/local read-model cache as the mechanism for rule 2 — correctly deferred for now (no messaging infra exists yet); only the *framing* of "wrong tool in principle" vs. "not yet justified by infra/consumer economics" needs tightening, not the conclusion.
- The suggested "invert the dependency so `training-planning-service` holds the local record" — already effectively true; the call direction in ADR-006 already asks the service that owns the data (`training-planning-service`), not the other way around.
- "Current version only" as the provisional default for what counts as "referenced" (rule 2) — reasonable low-risk default, correctly flagged as unresolved rather than quietly adopted; no change recommended, just confirm it stays visibly provisional.
